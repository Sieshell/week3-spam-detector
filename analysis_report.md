# Week 3 Project: Email Spam Detector - Analysis Report

## 1. Feature Engineering (Part 1)
In addition to the four default features (`word_count`, `exclamations`, `money_words`, `all_caps`), I added two custom features:
* **`urgency_words`**: Counts pushy terms like *"urgent", "now", "immediately", "hurry", "act"*. 
  * *Justification:* Spam emails manufacture false urgency to pressure recipients into acting before thinking critically.
* **`has_link`**: Checks for external link/action indicators (`"http"`, `"www"`, `"click"`). 
  * *Justification:* Spam messages rely heavily on external URLs to direct victims to phishing sites or storefronts, unlike routine everyday messages.

## 3. Cost of Errors & Model Interrogation (Parts 3.3 & 3.4)

### Which Error is Worse? (Part 3.3)
A **False Positive** (sending a legitimate email to the spam folder, such as an important job offer or meeting notice) is significantly worse than a **False Negative** (letting a spam email reach the inbox as a minor annoyance). Accuracy treats both errors equally, which is dangerous in real-world filters. To shift the balance and protect legitimate mail, one could raise the classification threshold (e.g., requiring a 90% spam probability instead of 50% before quarantining) or penalize false positives during training.

### What the Model Learned (Feature Weights - Part 3.4)

3. **`urgency_words`**: +0.75
4. **`exclamations`**: +0.42

* *Intuition Check:* Yes, `has_link`, `money_words`, and `urgency_words` having the strongest positive weights completely matches our intuition that spam relies on links, financial incentives, and pressure words.

## 4. Adversarial Attacks & Data Audit (Parts 3.5 & 3.6)

### Trying to Break It (Part 3.5)
I tested three adversarial emails against the model:
1. **Stealth Phishing Spam** (Spam disguised as a polite project invoice): **CAUGHT** (The link and context words still triggered the filter).
2. **Excited Legitimate Email from Mom** (All caps, exclamation marks, money words): **FOOLED THE FILTER** (Marked as spam due to high exclamation and money word counts).
3. **Subtle Crypto Romance Scam** (Spam with zero trigger words): **MISSED** (Passed into the inbox because it lacked standard keywords).

### Data Leakage Audit (Part 3.6)
Because the dataset was generated by taking 16 base emails and creating 10 slight random variations of each, **near-copies of all 16 template emails appeared in both the training set and the test set**. 
* *Conclusion:* The model effectively saw its own exam paper (its twin sibling templates) before taking the test. This explains the suspiciously high 100% accuracy and severely reduces our confidence in that metric. To fix this in the future, a `GroupShuffleSplit` should be used grouped by `template_id` so that entire template families are kept strictly out of the training set.

## 5. Conclusion & Real-World Trust
Before trusting this filter on real mail, I would need:
1. A truly independent test set free of data leakage.
2. Thousands of diverse, real-world emails rather than synthetic variations.
3. Natural Language Processing (NLP) embeddings (like TF-IDF or transformers) rather than simple keyword counting to catch sophisticated semantic scams.
